# Admit stopped contract checkpoints without resuming them

The two full-development model attempts stopped after eighteen and twenty-three responses. Their partial journals were durable, but the complete-run reader correctly refused them. Starting another complete run would repeat paid work before establishing whether the saved prefix was usable.

A separate read-only audit now checks that prefix. It requires a stopped live-run record, matching run/usage snapshots, the exact development dataset and an ordered, nonempty prefix of returned observations. Every input digest, current prompt/system fingerprint and output-schema fingerprint must match. Response shape is checked without gold-label scoring: a well-formed wrong answer stays eligible as a wrong answer. Unknown-usage or extra receipts, token-cap violations, arithmetic mismatches and missing retained campaign reservations are refused.

The audit reads bounded private files without following final symlinks and rechecks the bytes before reporting. It refuses torn lines, duplicate records and journals with a completion footer. It does not open credentials or call the provider, change a reservation, append a record, repair a partial file, compute an accuracy score or allow automatic resume. The existing complete-only replay boundary is unchanged. The independent CHAOSLAB wheel gains no SDK dependency; this operation belongs to the ATLAS model integration.

Both actual stopped runs passed: eighteen of sixty-one records in the first and twenty-three in the second. Those prefixes overlap. The recorded remaining-call caps are $2.103904 and $1.859264 under the historical price card, not spendable balances, invoices or fresh price approval. Both full reservations remain retained. The [aggregate record](../../reports/contract-checkpoints.json) contains the audit results and fingerprints, with no raw contracts, response text or document identifiers.

Thirty-one controlled cases passed, including prefix/order admission, malformed records, changed prompts, cost/usage uncertainty, duplicate campaign reservations, private-file checks, preservation of earlier CLI output and concurrent evidence changes. The native suite passed 833 tests, with eighteen optional PostgreSQL groups skipped. These are audit checks, not model-quality measurements.

Partial journals have no completion footer or independent provider response signature. Admission cannot establish that their response bytes were never edited before this audit. The fingerprints identify the evidence read now; they are not attestations. A future continuation must pin those fingerprints, retain source lineage, account for reused and newly requested work separately, serialize its execution and stop on uncertain usage. It also needs an explicit spending review. This change deliberately supplies none of that live execution yet.
