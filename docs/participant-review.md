# Watch someone use the projects

This is a session plan, not completed user research. No participant results have been collected by this release. Aim for three consenting people who have not built the projects. Record each person's relevant experience; include someone familiar with procurement or document review if available. Three sessions can reveal problems, but cannot establish adoption or a reliable time-saving estimate.

## Consent and privacy

Explain the purpose and ask permission before the session. Participation is optional, and the person can stop. Use fictional documents only. Take anonymous notes under P01, P02 and P03; keep recruitment details and raw notes out of Git. Ask separately before recording a screen or voice. Do not put contact details or participant quotes on the portfolio without specific consent. This plan does not authorize sending invitations.

## Before the session

Build the current release and check the free lab. Keep the local workbench ready with the fictional PDF/DOCX files so free-host capacity does not prevent the session. Record which environment was actually used. Give no explanation beyond: “This is a document-review tool and a separate tool for checking its answers. Please say what you think is happening.” The observer notes help requests rather than rescuing the participant immediately.

## ATLAS tasks

1. Ask the participant to explain what the opening page does and what they can try. Record their words before offering a definition.
2. Ask them to find out whether the supplier meets the recovery-time requirement. Let them start the sample, find the requirement and inspect both sources.
3. Ask them to record the next action they would take and explain why. A false approval of the eight-hour promise against the four-hour requirement is a critical error, even if the controls worked.
4. Reload and ask them to find the saved decision. In the local workbench, also ask them to locate the original document and audit entry. Record public and local persistence separately.
5. Ask what happens if the lab is busy. Let them inspect the clearly labeled saved example. Ask whether that example ran an analysis or saved a review.

## CHAOSLAB tasks

1. Ask why an answer can be wrong even when its citation matches the source.
2. Ask the participant to find one recorded failure and explain it in their own words.
3. Ask them to compare the 77.5% label figure with the 49.4% label-plus-complete-evidence figure. Check whether they mistakenly interpret either as RFP accuracy.
4. For a developer participant, provide the standalone setup instructions and ask them to run a supplied saved comparison. Observe whether they understand exit 1 as a recorded quality failure rather than an installation crash.

## Observation sheet

For each task record: start/end time if actually timed; completed unaided, completed with help, or not completed; every help request; wrong turns; final explanation; observed errors; environment/revision; and whether the answer was correct. Mark unavailable timing as unmeasured. Do not convert an observer's impression into a measurement.

Ask afterward: “What did you expect?”, “What was unclear?”, and “What would make this useful in your work?” Distinguish observed behavior from suggestions. Report all three sessions, including failures, with anonymous IDs and any relevant background. An improvement should address an observed issue and be checked with a new task or participant; do not train the same person on the answer and report that as first-visit success.

## Completion gate

Research remains pending until genuine sessions occur and consented observations exist. The report should include critical errors and participant limitations, not invented satisfaction scores, testimonials or adoption claims. The owner must approve any external contact and complete the sessions or supply authorized observations.
